What's New

Recent improvements to lehi31.com.

  1. September 16, 2026

    For leaders: a reminder to tell the organization a calling is sustained

    Once somebody is sustained, the president or leader they will be working with has to hear about it before they can start using them — and with nothing keeping track of that call, it was the part that quietly got missed. The sustained step now carries a box for it, "Organization leader informed", beside the clerk’s entry and the set-apart appointment. Ticking it records who made the call and when. It holds nothing up: the member can be set apart and serving with the box still unticked, and it stays on the step, waiting to be ticked, until somebody does. Until then it sits on the executive secretary’s list of what needs doing.

  2. September 15, 2026

    For leaders: sustaining and setting apart are one step

    A member is usually sustained and set apart within the same week, and often on the same day, so the calling no longer has to be moved along twice for one sitting’s work. Sustained is now a single step holding both: the date the calling was sustained, the clerk’s entry for it, the set-apart appointment if one is being booked, and then the date the member was set apart with the clerk’s entry for that. Both LCR boxes are on the step together, so whoever is doing the paperwork can finish all of it at once instead of ticking one, moving the calling on, and coming back for the other. Recording the setting apart is what marks the member as serving — neither box holds the calling up, and one still owing stays on the step to be ticked whenever it is done.

  3. September 13, 2026

    The whole site has been rebuilt to look and work the same everywhere

    Every page now sits in the same frame: the same header, the same menu, breadcrumbs that tell you where you are, the page title with its one main button beside it. Lists, tables, forms, drawers and confirmations were all rebuilt from a single set of parts, so a button looks and behaves the same whether you are recording a calling or logging a temple visit, and a table you could not read on a phone now stacks into rows you can. Everything was designed for the phone first and then widened for the desktop. Dark mode is a proper pair to the light theme rather than a shaded version of it, text sits at a readable contrast throughout, and the keyboard can reach every control — including a Skip to content link for anyone using a screen reader. Nothing moved and nothing was removed; it should simply be easier to find, read and tap.

  4. September 9, 2026

    For leaders: the menu is grouped by the work, and shows what needs you

    Ward Business had grown into fifteen tools behind one label, listed alphabetically, so the Sunday program sat next to room setup and the page you opened rarely held the one thing you came for. It is now three sections named for what you are doing and ordered by how often you reach for them — This Sunday for the program, the pulpit, and who conducts; People for callings, the directory, and the lists you text; Setup for the things you arrange once and rarely touch. The rest of the menu is split into Look up and Run the ward, so the pages every member uses no longer read as the top of an admin menu. And a new Needs you sits above Home carrying a live count of what is actually waiting on you — approvals, appointments to schedule, sustainings to record, open Sunday assignments — with the same count on each section it lives in, so you can see there is something to do from whatever page you are on. It is only there when something is waiting.

  5. September 9, 2026

    Ward music can set the hymns without touching the rest

    The ward music callings — the music coordinator, the organist, and the chorister — can now open the program planner and set the sacrament hymns for any Sunday that has been started, and they see only the Music tab. Speakers, prayers, and planning stay with the bishopric, so the music can be kept current by the people who know it without opening the rest of the program.

  6. September 9, 2026

    An event can belong to more than one organization

    A combined activity — a joint Young Men and Young Women night, say — can now be tagged with both organizations instead of just one. It shows both badges on the calendar, and it turns up whether you filter by either organization, so it no longer goes missing for half the ward. Events with a single organization look exactly as before.

  7. September 6, 2026

    For leaders: a shorter approvals screen, and event details you can see

    The calendar approvals tab opened with every published event laid out — well over a hundred of them — with the handful actually waiting on the bishopric buried underneath. Published now starts folded away behind its count, and any of the groups can be folded or opened by tapping its heading, so the screen opens on the work rather than the archive. When importing from the church calendar, an event’s description now shows on the row itself instead of only appearing, unlabelled, after opening it — so you can tell what an event is before deciding to import it. The quick links on the home page are grouped by what you came to do rather than listed alphabetically, and the pages for calendar approvals and ordinance progress have been retired: approvals live on the calendar page itself, which is where the work already was.

  8. September 5, 2026

    Your phone can tell you when something is posted

    The site can now send a notification to your phone when a new announcement goes up, when an event is added to the calendar or moved to a different day, and on Saturday evening when the program for the coming Sunday is ready. Turn them on under your profile, where each of those is its own switch — you can take the Sunday program and leave the rest. They arrive whether or not the site is open, and they keep coming even if you are signed out, so nothing is missed because a sign-in quietly lapsed. Add the site to your home screen first if you are on an iPhone; notifications only work once it is installed.

  9. September 5, 2026

    The Sunday schedules are on the front page

    Sunday Lessons and the class schedule now sit on the front page, alongside the calendar and the temple challenge, so you can check what your class is studying or where it meets without hunting through the menu. The class schedule no longer asks you to sign in either — a visitor, or a member on a borrowed phone, can look up which room a class is in.

  10. August 31, 2026

    See what every class is studying this Sunday

    There is a new Sunday Lessons page that says what each class is studying, week by week, with a link straight to the lesson. It follows the right source for each group — Come, Follow Me for Sunday School and Primary, For the Strength of Youth for the Young Women and the Aaronic Priesthood quorums, and the assigned conference talk for Elders Quorum and Relief Society — and each organisation has its own page, so you can go straight to yours rather than reading past everybody else’s. The index is grouped by age, and Sunday School appears under both adults and youth, since both meet in it.

  11. August 30, 2026

    The building map sits under the sacrament program

    The meetinghouse map now appears below the sacrament program, so when the program says a class is meeting after the block you can see where to go without leaving the page.

  12. August 30, 2026

    Tongan hymns from the new hymnbook

    The 1000-series Tongan hymns from the new hymnbook can now be chosen when a program is planned, so a Tongan hymn is named properly in the program instead of being typed in by hand.

  13. August 29, 2026

    Find where your class meets on a building map

    The class schedule now shows a map of the meetinghouse, so you can see at a glance where your Sunday School class or quorum meets. Tap a room to see which classes are there, and pinch or double-tap to zoom in on your wing. Every classroom and the full class list is there — Sunday School, the quorums and Relief Society in English and Tongan, Young Women, Aaronic Priesthood, and each Primary class — and you no longer need to sign in to look it up.

  14. August 29, 2026

    Leaders set a class’s room where they edit the class

    Setting where a class meets used to be a separate screen; it now happens on the class itself, with the building map in view and a room chosen for each period from a searchable list. A large class can be split across two rooms, and if a room is already taken that hour the site asks whether to combine the two classes there or pick another — so two groups are never sent to the same door by accident. The class list now names the rooms each class uses instead of only saying which hour it meets.

  15. August 25, 2026

    The callings board opens a stage at a time on a phone

    The board lists every step of the calling workflow, then laid all of them out underneath — on a phone that meant scrolling past a dozen stages of cards to read what should have been a summary. Tapping a step now opens just that step, right where you tapped, and closes the one you were in. On a computer nothing changes: every stage stays laid out below and a step still jumps to it.

  16. August 25, 2026

    The missionaries are named from one place

    The Missionaries card on Ward Info and beside the sacrament program was written into the site itself, and had gone three companionships out of date — it still named elders who left the ward long ago. It now reads the same list the missionaries page manages, so adding a new elder or recording a transfer updates every page at once, with no code change and nothing to remember to keep in step.

  17. August 24, 2026

    Missionaries have a profile, and a record of who served here

    Tapping a missionary now opens their profile, where the photo is large enough to actually recognise someone you have met once, alongside their mission, service dates, and buttons to call or text the missionaries serving here. Adding that photo used to mean picking a file already on your device and using it exactly as it was — often a many-megabyte camera picture. The missionary form now has the same Choose photo and Take photo buttons as a member record, opens the camera straight from the form on a phone, and lets you frame the square yourself before it is saved. When a missionary assigned to our ward is transferred out there is now a date for the day they left, so they come off the page without their record being thrown away — the ward can still look back and see who was serving here.

  18. August 21, 2026

    Announcements come before the opening hymn again

    The conducting script had announcements after the opening hymn and prayer. That was wrong: Handbook 29.2.1.1 puts the welcome first, then announcements kept to a minimum, then the opening hymn and prayer, and only then ward business. The script now reads in that order. Opening the cheat sheet directly also used to say no program had been built even when one had — it was not loading the program for itself, and now it does.

  19. August 20, 2026

    Take a member photo with your phone

    Adding a profile photo meant finding one already saved on your device. On a phone there is now a Take photo button beside Choose photo that opens the camera straight from the form. Either way the photo is shrunk before you see it, so the crop stays quick even on a large camera picture, and you still frame it yourself before it is saved.

  20. August 18, 2026

    People are named the way they are known

    A member who goes by something other than the name on the roster now reads that way wherever they are named — the calling roster, the callings board, member lists, and the ordinance pages. Searching still finds them under either name, so someone recorded as Grant turns up whether you type Grant or Skip. Their own record is the exception: it keeps the name as filed and shows the nickname underneath, so there is one place that always answers what the roster says.

  21. August 18, 2026

    A release now starts with a conversation

    Handbook 30.6 asks a leader to meet with a member personally and tell them of a release before it is announced. Until now a release went straight from being started to being read over the pulpit, with nothing recording whether anyone had spoken to the person. There is a step in between for the day that meeting was held, and a release cannot be put on a Sunday program until it is filled in. Where there is good reason there was no meeting, the bishopric can say so in writing, and that reason stays with the calling. The home page also names the right meeting now: a release is announced where the person was sustained, so one sustained in a class no longer reads as sacrament meeting.

  22. August 11, 2026

    Young Women classes have new names

    The First Presidency announced new names for the Young Women age groups in April — Builders of Faith for ages 12 and 13, Messengers of Hope for 14 and 15, and Gatherers of Light for 16 and older. Those names now appear wherever a class is named, in place of Pearls, Rubies, and Diamonds. Nothing needs to be re-tagged: events already assigned to a class keep their place on the calendar, and only the wording changed.

  23. August 9, 2026

    Training notes live under Resources

    Resources has a new Trainings section holding notes from recent trainings — the August advisory training on being disciples of Christ, and The Huddle from stake correlation. Each one is grouped by theme so it can be read a piece at a time rather than as a transcript, and later trainings will be added in the same place.

  24. August 8, 2026

    The ordinance list opens on what needs attention

    The next-ordinance page used to open with every group expanded, so it began with hundreds of names and buried the handful that actually needed the bishopric. Groups now stay closed unless something falls within three months, and the page covers the next twelve months by default. Adults appear once a leader adds them, since age alone says nothing about what comes next, while youth are still worked out automatically so nobody is quietly missed.

  25. August 7, 2026

    See who is coming up on their next ordinance

    A new page for the bishopric and ward clerk shows each member and the next ordinance ahead of them, worked out from date of birth alone. LCR stays the system of record for ordinance dates, so nothing here is copied from it. Leaders can add what a birthday cannot tell you — whether a step is scheduled or already reached, a note to remember, and the small preparations a member has been invited to. Those preparations follow the member, so they do not start over when a young man moves from teacher to priest.

  26. August 5, 2026

    Set the speaking order from the Speakers tab

    Choosing who speaks first meant leaving the Speakers tab and opening the program itself. The speakers are already numbered in the drawer, and those numbers can now be changed right there — the hymns and notes around them keep their place.

  27. August 3, 2026

    A conducting script you can read straight through

    The conducting script on the sacrament program used to hand off to a bare list of names, leaving whoever was conducting to improvise between the sacrament, the speakers, and the closing hymn. There is now wording for every handoff, and it follows whatever is actually on the program — first speaker, next speaker, concluding speaker, the musical number, the rest hymn, and fast Sunday testimonies.

  28. August 3, 2026

    The sacrament is an ordinance

    Following recent direction from the Church, the conducting script now refers to the ordinance of the sacrament throughout, and speaks of sacrament meeting as a meeting rather than a service.

  29. August 3, 2026

    Leadership meetings stay off the pulpit

    Events can be marked "For leaders only" when you create or edit them. Ward council, correlation, and leadership training are then left out of the announcements on the conducting script, so they are not read to the congregation — they still appear on the calendar as normal. The standing Sunday block no longer shows up under "coming up" either, since announcing sacrament meeting during sacrament meeting is just noise.

  30. August 3, 2026

    The script never invents names

    A sacrament program that had not been filled in yet still rendered as a complete script, prefilled with the usual bishopric, chorister, and organist — so someone conducting could read out names nobody had assigned. The script now says plainly when there is no program to read from, and shows only what is genuinely on the program.

  31. August 2, 2026

    Fill in music and prayers without opening every Sunday

    The Sacrament Programs page has new Music and Prayers tabs alongside Speakers. Each lists the Sundays in a rolling three-month window with what is already assigned, so the gaps are obvious at a glance. Tap a Sunday to set its hymns, add a musical number or rest hymn, or name who is praying — all without opening one program at a time.

  32. August 2, 2026

    The middle of the program is now one list

    Speakers and musical numbers used to be split into a group before the rest hymn and a group after it, so every speaker had to be filed on one side or the other. That is gone — the middle of the program is a single list you can reorder freely, and the rest hymn is just another item in it. You can also drop in a plain note for anything that does not fit the usual slots.

  33. August 2, 2026

    Shorter event titles, everywhere

    The shorter display titles you set on church calendar events now show up in every place an event is read — event pages, the day list, search results, and shared links — not just the calendar grid.

  34. August 2, 2026

    Member names read the way you would say them

    The membership export packs middle names into the first-name field, so roughly one member in ten was showing up with all three names. The directory, member picker, and calling views now show given and last name only. Full names are unchanged behind the scenes, and search still finds a middle name if you type it.

  35. August 1, 2026

    Callings scoped to your organization

    Auxiliary leaders now see the callings for their own organization family rather than the whole ward — Young Men leadership sees Young Men presidency, teachers, and deacons together, for example. The bishopric, clerks, and stake leaders keep the ward-wide view.

  36. August 1, 2026

    The elders quorum presidency runs its own callings

    Elders quorum callings can now be extended, presented, and set apart by the quorum presidency instead of routing through the bishopric. Those callings are marked so it is clear at a glance who is handling them.

  37. August 1, 2026

    No more duplicate calling proposals

    A member can only have one proposal in flight for the same calling at a time, so the same name no longer shows up twice on the board.

  38. July 31, 2026

    Sustainings go to the right meeting

    Some callings are sustained in sacrament meeting and some are sustained in class or quorum — and a few, like an Aaronic Priesthood quorum presidency, need both. The app now tracks which applies and puts each sustaining on the right list, following the Handbook.

  39. July 31, 2026

    Fix a calling name after proposing it

    A calling name can now be corrected while the proposal is still in flight, by the bishopric or by whoever submitted it — no need to start over. The callings board also reads properly on a phone.

  40. July 29, 2026

    See who has spoken in sacrament meeting

    The Sacrament Programs page has a new Speakers tab for the bishopric and clerks. It shows who is scheduled to speak on upcoming Sundays and who has spoken over the past three months, with a summary of each person and when they last spoke — so it is easy to see who has already been asked recently. Older periods can be browsed a quarter at a time.

  41. July 28, 2026

    Shorter names for church calendar events

    Events pulled from the church calendar arrive with the church's calendar name tacked onto the title, like "General Conference Saturday Morning Session - Churchwide Events". You can now give any of them a shorter display title — on the import review page or when editing the event — and that wording is what shows up on the calendar, in shared links, and in subscribed calendars. The church's original title is kept behind the scenes, so searching for it still works and future syncs never flag the event as changed.

  42. July 27, 2026

    Ward business, Sunday by Sunday

    Sacrament and class business — sustainings, releases, baptisms, blessings, advancements, and move-ins — is now kept per Sunday instead of overwritten each week. Sustaining or releasing a calling records it on the right Sunday automatically, and after the meeting a one-tap confirmation marks what was actually announced; anything skipped rolls forward to the next week. Leadership can open any past Sunday and see exactly what was conducted.

  43. July 25, 2026

    Schedule months of Sundays at once

    The Sacrament Programs page can now set up future Sundays in one step. Choose a date range and every Sunday in it starts with the usual presiding, chorister, organist, prayer and hymn assignments already filled in — fast Sundays marked, General Conference weekends skipped, and conducting left for the bishopric to assign.

  44. July 25, 2026

    Sacrament programs, past and future

    Every sacrament program is now kept by date instead of being replaced each week. The program page still shows the upcoming Sunday, and the bishopric can look back at past meetings or fill in future Sundays ahead of time from the new Sacrament Programs page.

  45. July 24, 2026

    Sync the ward calendar to your own

    A new Subscribe button on the Calendar page connects Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook. Ward events flow into your personal calendar and stay up to date automatically — including changes and cancellations.

  46. July 24, 2026

  47. July 24, 2026

    Calling releases, start to finish

    Releasing someone from a calling is now a tracked workflow: begin the release, record when it was announced in sacrament meeting, and report it to LCR. Existing callings that predate the app can be recorded directly as active so they can be released too.

  48. July 24, 2026

    The signed-in home page now leads with Quick Links and your action items, with more destinations one tap away.

  49. July 23, 2026

  50. July 22, 2026

  51. July 20, 2026

  53. July 19, 2026
