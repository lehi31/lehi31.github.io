What's New

Recent improvements to lehi31.com.

  1. 2026
  2. September 21, 2026

    A place to raise a request and decide it together

    For leaders

    There is a new Requests space for anything that needs the bishopric to decide — you describe what you are asking, and all three seats vote to approve or decline.

    • Each request carries its own discussion, in spaces that widen out from the bishopric alone, to the clerks and executive secretary, to whoever raised it, and then to the presidencies of the organizations it concerns
    • When you raise one you tag the organization or organizations it is about, and only the leaders over those organizations you have access to
    • The bishopric can enter a decision on a counselor’s behalf when it was given verbally, marked as a proxy so the record stays honest
    • Every request keeps the conclusion that was reached, so it becomes a searchable, sortable archive of what was asked, who was involved, and what was agreed
    • Push notifications tell the people who need to follow up; each request shows whether it is waiting on you or has unread discussion, with a way to mark it read
  3. September 21, 2026

    The calendar moves between months with a swipe

    On a phone or tablet the month calendar now changes month when you swipe it sideways — left for the next month, right for the previous — so you can move through the year with your thumb instead of reaching for the arrows. The arrows are still there, and swiping never gets in the way of scrolling the page or tapping a day.

  4. September 21, 2026

    Search the menu

    The menu has a search box at the top. Type a word and it flattens the whole navigation — every page and every page tucked inside another — into a short list you can jump straight into, so a page three levels deep is one search away instead of several taps.

  5. September 16, 2026

    A reminder to tell the organization a calling is sustained

    For leaders

    Once somebody is sustained, the leader they will be working with has to hear about it before they can start using them — the step that quietly got missed because nothing kept track of that call.

    • The sustained step now carries an "Organization leader informed" box beside the clerk’s entry and the set-apart appointment; ticking it records who made the call and when
    • It holds nothing up — the member can be set apart and serving with the box still unticked
    • Until someone ticks it, the reminder stays on the step and on the executive secretary’s list of what needs doing
  6. September 15, 2026

    Sustaining and setting apart are one step

    For leaders

    A member is usually sustained and set apart within the same week, and often on the same day, so the calling no longer has to be moved along twice for one sitting’s work.

    • Sustained is now a single step holding both: the date sustained, the clerk’s entry for it, the set-apart appointment if one is being booked, and the date set apart with its clerk’s entry
    • Both LCR boxes sit on the step together, so the paperwork can be finished at once instead of ticking one, moving the calling on, and coming back for the other
    • Recording the setting apart is what marks the member as serving; neither box holds the calling up, and one still owing stays on the step to be ticked whenever it is done
  7. September 13, 2026

    The whole site has been rebuilt to look and work the same everywhere

    Every page now sits in the same frame — the same header and menu, breadcrumbs that tell you where you are, and the page title with its one main button beside it.

    • Lists, tables, forms, drawers and confirmations were rebuilt from a single set of parts, so a button behaves the same whether you are recording a calling or logging a temple visit, and a table you could not read on a phone now stacks into rows
    • Everything was designed for the phone first and then widened for the desktop
    • Dark mode is now a proper pair to the light theme, text sits at a readable contrast throughout, and the keyboard can reach every control — including a Skip to content link for screen readers
    • Nothing moved and nothing was removed; it should simply be easier to find, read and tap
  8. September 9, 2026

    The menu is grouped by the work, and shows what needs you

    For leaders

    Ward Business had grown into fifteen tools behind one label, listed alphabetically, so the page you opened rarely held the one thing you came for.

    • It is now three sections ordered by how often you reach for them — This Sunday for the program, the pulpit, and who conducts; People for callings, the directory, and the lists you text; Setup for the things you arrange once
    • The rest of the menu is split into Look up and Run the ward, so the pages every member uses no longer read as the top of an admin menu
    • A new Needs you sits above Home carrying a live count of what is waiting on you — approvals, appointments to schedule, sustainings to record, open Sunday assignments — and it is only there when something is waiting
  9. September 9, 2026

    Ward music can set the hymns without touching the rest

    The ward music callings — the music coordinator, the organist, and the chorister — can now open the program planner and set the sacrament hymns for any Sunday that has been started.

    • They see only the Music tab; speakers, prayers, and planning stay with the bishopric
    • The music can be kept current by the people who know it, without opening the rest of the program
  10. September 9, 2026

    An event can belong to more than one organization

    A combined activity — a joint Young Men and Young Women night, say — can now be tagged with both organizations instead of just one. It shows both badges on the calendar, and it turns up whether you filter by either organization, so it no longer goes missing for half the ward. Events with a single organization look exactly as before.

  11. September 6, 2026

    A shorter approvals screen, and event details you can see

    For leaders

    The calendar approvals tab used to open with every published event laid out — well over a hundred of them — with the handful actually waiting on the bishopric buried underneath.

    • Published now starts folded away behind its count, and any of the groups can be folded or opened by tapping its heading, so the screen opens on the work rather than the archive
    • When importing from the church calendar, an event’s description now shows on the row itself instead of only after opening it, so you can tell what it is before deciding to import it
    • The quick links on the home page are grouped by what you came to do rather than listed alphabetically
    • The separate calendar approvals and ordinance progress pages have been retired — approvals now live on the calendar page itself, where the work already was
  12. September 5, 2026

    Your phone can tell you when something is posted

    The site can now send a notification to your phone when something is posted, and each kind is its own switch under your profile, so you can take the Sunday program and leave the rest.

    • Notifications can fire for a new announcement, an event added or moved to a different day, and on Saturday evening when the coming Sunday’s program is ready
    • They arrive whether or not the site is open, and keep coming even if you are signed out, so nothing is missed because a sign-in quietly lapsed
    • On an iPhone, add the site to your home screen first — notifications only work once it is installed
  13. September 5, 2026

    The Sunday schedules are on the front page

    Sunday Lessons and the class schedule now sit on the front page, alongside the calendar and the temple challenge, so you can check what your class is studying or where it meets without hunting through the menu.

    • The class schedule no longer asks you to sign in — a visitor, or a member on a borrowed phone, can look up which room a class is in
  14. August 31, 2026

    See what every class is studying this Sunday

    There is a new Sunday Lessons page that says what each class is studying, week by week, with a link straight to the lesson.

    • It follows the right source for each group — Come, Follow Me for Sunday School and Primary, For the Strength of Youth for the Young Women and the Aaronic Priesthood quorums, and the assigned conference talk for Elders Quorum and Relief Society
    • Each organisation has its own page, so you can go straight to yours rather than reading past everybody else’s
    • The index is grouped by age, and Sunday School appears under both adults and youth, since both meet in it
  15. August 30, 2026

    The building map sits under the sacrament program

    The meetinghouse map now appears below the sacrament program, so when the program says a class is meeting after the block you can see where to go without leaving the page.

  16. August 30, 2026

    Tongan hymns from the new hymnbook

    The 1000-series Tongan hymns from the new hymnbook can now be chosen when a program is planned, so a Tongan hymn is named properly in the program instead of being typed in by hand.

  17. August 29, 2026

    Find where your class meets on a building map

    The class schedule now shows a map of the meetinghouse, so you can see at a glance where your Sunday School class or quorum meets.

    • Tap a room to see which classes are there, and pinch or double-tap to zoom in on your wing
    • Every classroom and the full class list is there — Sunday School, the quorums and Relief Society in English and Tongan, Young Women, Aaronic Priesthood, and each Primary class
    • You no longer need to sign in to look it up
  18. August 29, 2026

    Leaders set a class’s room where they edit the class

    For leaders

    Setting where a class meets used to be a separate screen; it now happens on the class itself, with the building map in view and a room chosen for each period from a searchable list.

    • A large class can be split across two rooms
    • If a room is already taken that hour, the site asks whether to combine the two classes there or pick another — so two groups are never sent to the same door by accident
    • The class list now names the rooms each class uses instead of only saying which hour it meets
  19. August 25, 2026

    The callings board opens a stage at a time on a phone

    For leaders

    The callings board used to lay every stage out underneath, so on a phone you scrolled past a dozen stages of cards to read what should have been a summary.

    • Tapping a step now opens just that step, right where you tapped, and closes the one you were in
    • On a computer nothing changes — every stage stays laid out below, and a step still jumps to it
  20. August 25, 2026

    The missionaries are named from one place

    The Missionaries card on Ward Info and beside the sacrament program was written into the site itself, and had gone three companionships out of date — still naming elders who left the ward long ago.

    • It now reads the same list the missionaries page manages
    • Adding a new elder or recording a transfer updates every page at once, with no code change and nothing to remember to keep in step
  21. August 24, 2026

    Missionaries have a profile, and a record of who served here

    Tapping a missionary now opens their profile, where the photo is large enough to actually recognise someone you have met once, alongside their mission, service dates, and buttons to call or text the missionaries serving here.

    • The missionary form now has the same Choose photo and Take photo buttons as a member record, opens the camera straight from the form on a phone, and lets you frame the square yourself before it is saved
    • When a missionary assigned to our ward is transferred out, a leaving date takes them off the page without throwing their record away, so the ward can still look back and see who was serving here
  22. August 21, 2026

    Announcements come before the opening hymn again

    The conducting script had announcements after the opening hymn and prayer, which was wrong — Handbook 29.2.1.1 puts the welcome first, then announcements kept to a minimum, then the opening hymn and prayer, and only then ward business.

    • The script now reads in that order
    • Opening the cheat sheet directly used to say no program had been built even when one had — it was not loading the program for itself, and now it does
  23. August 20, 2026

    Take a member photo with your phone

    Adding a profile photo meant finding one already saved on your device; on a phone there is now a Take photo button beside Choose photo that opens the camera straight from the form.

    • Either way the photo is shrunk before you see it, so the crop stays quick even on a large camera picture
    • You still frame it yourself before it is saved
  24. August 18, 2026

    People are named the way they are known

    A member who goes by something other than the name on the roster now reads that way wherever they are named — the calling roster, the callings board, member lists, and the ordinance pages.

    • Searching still finds them under either name, so someone recorded as Grant turns up whether you type Grant or Skip
    • Their own record is the exception — it keeps the name as filed and shows the nickname underneath, so there is one place that always answers what the roster says
  25. August 18, 2026

    A release now starts with a conversation

    For leaders

    Handbook 30.6 asks a leader to meet with a member personally and tell them of a release before it is announced, but until now a release went straight from being started to being read over the pulpit, with nothing recording whether anyone had spoken to the person.

    • There is now a step for the day that meeting was held, and a release cannot be put on a Sunday program until it is filled in
    • Where there is good reason there was no meeting, the bishopric can say so in writing, and that reason stays with the calling
    • The home page now names the right meeting — a release is announced where the person was sustained, so one sustained in a class no longer reads as sacrament meeting
  26. August 11, 2026

    Young Women classes have new names

    The First Presidency announced new names for the Young Women age groups in April, and those names now appear wherever a class is named, in place of Pearls, Rubies, and Diamonds.

    • Builders of Faith for ages 12 and 13
    • Messengers of Hope for ages 14 and 15
    • Gatherers of Light for ages 16 and older
    • Nothing needs to be re-tagged — events already assigned to a class keep their place on the calendar, and only the wording changed
  27. August 9, 2026

    Training notes live under Resources

    Resources has a new Trainings section holding notes from recent trainings — the August advisory training on being disciples of Christ, and The Huddle from stake correlation. Each one is grouped by theme so it can be read a piece at a time rather than as a transcript, and later trainings will be added in the same place.

  28. August 8, 2026

    The ordinance list opens on what needs attention

    For leaders

    The next-ordinance page used to open with every group expanded, so it began with hundreds of names and buried the handful that actually needed the bishopric.

    • Groups now stay closed unless something falls within three months, and the page covers the next twelve months by default
    • Adults appear once a leader adds them, since age alone says nothing about what comes next
    • Youth are still worked out automatically, so nobody is quietly missed
  29. August 7, 2026

    See who is coming up on their next ordinance

    For leaders

    A new page for the bishopric and ward clerk shows each member and the next ordinance ahead of them, worked out from date of birth alone.

    • LCR stays the system of record for ordinance dates, so nothing here is copied from it
    • Leaders can add what a birthday cannot tell you — whether a step is scheduled or already reached, a note to remember, and the small preparations a member has been invited to
    • Those preparations follow the member, so they do not start over when a young man moves from teacher to priest
  30. August 5, 2026

    Set the speaking order from the Speakers tab

    Choosing who speaks first meant leaving the Speakers tab and opening the program itself. The speakers are already numbered in the drawer, and those numbers can now be changed right there — the hymns and notes around them keep their place.

  31. August 3, 2026

    A conducting script you can read straight through

    The conducting script on the sacrament program used to hand off to a bare list of names, leaving whoever was conducting to improvise between the sacrament, the speakers, and the closing hymn.

    • There is now wording for every handoff
    • It follows whatever is actually on the program — first speaker, next speaker, concluding speaker, the musical number, the rest hymn, and fast Sunday testimonies
  32. August 3, 2026

    The sacrament is an ordinance

    Following recent direction from the Church, the conducting script now refers to the ordinance of the sacrament throughout, and speaks of sacrament meeting as a meeting rather than a service.

  33. August 3, 2026

    Leadership meetings stay off the pulpit

    Events can be marked "For leaders only" when you create or edit them, and are then left out of the announcements on the conducting script so they are not read to the congregation.

    • Ward council, correlation, and leadership training still appear on the calendar as normal
    • The standing Sunday block no longer shows up under "coming up" either, since announcing sacrament meeting during sacrament meeting is just noise
  34. August 3, 2026

    The script never invents names

    A sacrament program that had not been filled in yet still rendered as a complete script, prefilled with the usual bishopric, chorister, and organist — so someone conducting could read out names nobody had assigned. The script now says plainly when there is no program to read from, and shows only what is genuinely on the program.

  35. August 2, 2026

    Fill in music and prayers without opening every Sunday

    The Sacrament Programs page has new Music and Prayers tabs alongside Speakers. Each lists the Sundays in a rolling three-month window with what is already assigned, so the gaps are obvious at a glance. Tap a Sunday to set its hymns, add a musical number or rest hymn, or name who is praying — all without opening one program at a time.

  36. August 2, 2026

    The middle of the program is now one list

    Speakers and musical numbers used to be split into a group before the rest hymn and a group after it, so every speaker had to be filed on one side or the other.

    • The middle of the program is now a single list you can reorder freely, and the rest hymn is just another item in it
    • You can also drop in a plain note for anything that does not fit the usual slots
  37. August 2, 2026

    Shorter event titles, everywhere

    The shorter display titles you set on church calendar events now show up in every place an event is read — event pages, the day list, search results, and shared links — not just the calendar grid.

  38. August 2, 2026

    Member names read the way you would say them

    The membership export packs middle names into the first-name field, so roughly one member in ten was showing up with all three names. The directory, member picker, and calling views now show given and last name only. Full names are unchanged behind the scenes, and search still finds a middle name if you type it.

  39. August 1, 2026

    Callings scoped to your organization

    For leaders

    Auxiliary leaders now see the callings for their own organization family rather than the whole ward — Young Men leadership sees Young Men presidency, teachers, and deacons together, for example. The bishopric, clerks, and stake leaders keep the ward-wide view.

  40. August 1, 2026

    The elders quorum presidency runs its own callings

    For leaders

    Elders quorum callings can now be extended, presented, and set apart by the quorum presidency instead of routing through the bishopric. Those callings are marked so it is clear at a glance who is handling them.

  41. August 1, 2026

    No more duplicate calling proposals

    For leaders

    A member can only have one proposal in flight for the same calling at a time, so the same name no longer shows up twice on the board.

  42. July 31, 2026

    Sustainings go to the right meeting

    For leaders

    Some callings are sustained in sacrament meeting and some are sustained in class or quorum — and a few, like an Aaronic Priesthood quorum presidency, need both. The app now tracks which applies and puts each sustaining on the right list, following the Handbook.

  43. July 31, 2026

    Fix a calling name after proposing it

    For leaders

    A calling name can now be corrected while the proposal is still in flight, by the bishopric or by whoever submitted it — no need to start over. The callings board also reads properly on a phone.

  44. July 29, 2026

    See who has spoken in sacrament meeting

    For leaders

    The Sacrament Programs page has a new Speakers tab for the bishopric and clerks, showing who is scheduled to speak on upcoming Sundays and who has spoken over the past three months.

    • A summary of each person and when they last spoke makes it easy to see who has already been asked recently
    • Older periods can be browsed a quarter at a time
  45. July 28, 2026

    Shorter names for church calendar events

    Events pulled from the church calendar arrive with the church’s calendar name tacked onto the title, like “General Conference Saturday Morning Session - Churchwide Events”. You can now give any of them a shorter display title.

    • Set the shorter title on the import review page or when editing the event
    • That wording is what shows up on the calendar, in shared links, and in subscribed calendars
    • The church’s original title is kept behind the scenes, so searching for it still works and future syncs never flag the event as changed
  46. July 27, 2026

    Ward business, Sunday by Sunday

    For leaders

    Sacrament and class business — sustainings, releases, baptisms, blessings, advancements, and move-ins — is now kept per Sunday instead of overwritten each week.

    • Sustaining or releasing a calling records it on the right Sunday automatically
    • After the meeting a one-tap confirmation marks what was actually announced, and anything skipped rolls forward to the next week
    • Leadership can open any past Sunday and see exactly what was conducted
  47. July 25, 2026

    Schedule months of Sundays at once

    For leaders

    The Sacrament Programs page can now set up future Sundays in one step — choose a date range and every Sunday in it starts with the usual assignments already filled in.

    • Presiding, chorister, organist, prayer and hymn assignments are prefilled
    • Fast Sundays are marked and General Conference weekends skipped
    • Conducting is left for the bishopric to assign
  48. July 25, 2026

    Sacrament programs, past and future

    Every sacrament program is now kept by date instead of being replaced each week. The program page still shows the upcoming Sunday, and the bishopric can look back at past meetings or fill in future Sundays ahead of time from the new Sacrament Programs page.

  49. July 24, 2026

    Sync the ward calendar to your own

    A new Subscribe button on the Calendar page connects Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook. Ward events flow into your personal calendar and stay up to date automatically — including changes and cancellations.

  50. July 24, 2026

    Church calendar changes are flagged

    For leaders

    When an event imported from the church calendar changes at the source — a new time, location, or title — the import review now shows a "Needs Update" tab with a side-by-side comparison, so nothing drifts out of date unnoticed.

  51. July 24, 2026

    Calling releases, start to finish

    For leaders

  52. July 24, 2026

  53. July 23, 2026

  54. July 22, 2026
